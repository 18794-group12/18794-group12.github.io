**MetaTrace is the working title of our 18-794 Group 12 project.** We study which signals reveal the generator behind an AI-generated image.

## the story

Imagine an attribution model that labels anime images as generator A and photographic portraits as generator B. It may score well while learning appearance shortcuts.

Now ask both generators to draw the same scene. Does the classifier still identify their sources? What happens after compression, resizing, or a change in style?

This is the distinction we want to study. **Content** describes subjects and semantic cues. **Generator fingerprints** are source-related patterns in textures, residuals, frequencies, or learned features.

Reliable attribution should remain useful when the depicted subject changes. Our goal is to measure dependence on content, identify useful source signals, and test their robustness.

## the research logic

1. **Diagnose:** compare attribution under controlled prompt and source conditions.
2. **Locate:** test RGB, forensic features, and pretrained representations with simple probes.
3. **Learn:** encourage source consistency across different prompts and image transformations.
4. **Stress-test:** measure performance under edits, new data, and adversarial perturbations.

The core deliverable is a controlled comparison of attribution signals. Representation learning and robustness experiments build on that comparison.

## data

We will start with [DRAGON-R](https://huggingface.co/datasets/lesc-unifi/dragon): approximately 25,000 training images and 10,000 test images from 25 generators. Its shared prompts support comparisons across sources.

[Defactify / MS COCOAI](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Rajarshi-Roy-research/Defactify_Image_Dataset) provides an independent extension: 96,000 images across real photographs and five synthetic sources.
